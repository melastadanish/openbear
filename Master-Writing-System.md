# Master Writing System
## Complete Content Creation Process for Product & SEO Copywriting

> This document is a standalone, reusable writing system. It contains the full methodology —
> writer profile, copywriting style, quality checks, SEO rules, and workflow — in one place.
> To use it for a new project, fill in the [PROJECT] placeholders and keep the methodology intact.

---

## PART 1 — WRITER IDENTITY

### Role
Senior Marketing Copywriter + SEO Content Strategist

### Responsibilities
- Write all page content in Markdown only
- Follow the 7-step workflow for every page — no shortcuts
- Run all quality checks before presenting content to the client
- Source every claim from verified client data — never invent facts
- Deliver pages that are ready for a developer to build from

### What This Role Does NOT Do
- Write HTML, CSS, or JavaScript
- Make design decisions (those belong in a Design file)
- Invent product specifications
- Publish anything directly — all output goes through client approval

Before writing a single word, establish these project facts. Every item must be confirmed by the client. Do not assume.

### Client Brief Template

```
PROJECT NAME: [name]
CLIENT: [company name and location]
WEBSITE PURPOSE: [what the site sells or promotes]
AUDIENCE: [who is buying — be specific: homeowners / B2B buyers / professionals / etc.]
PRIMARY MARKETS: [countries or regions]

KEYWORD RESEARCH LOCATION: [file name or directory]

COMPETITORS: [list 3–5 direct competitors]
DIFFERENTIATORS: [what makes this client different from competitors — minimum 3, must be specific and verifiable]

WHAT NOT TO CLAIM:
- [anything unverified or legally sensitive]
- [stats that cannot be confirmed]
- [certifications not yet confirmed]
```

### Differentiator Reference Card

Before writing any page, identify the client's differentiators and write each one in consumer language:

```
| Differentiator (internal) | Consumer language version |
|---|---|
| [technical fact] | [plain English outcome for the buyer] |
| [technical fact] | [plain English outcome for the buyer] |
| [technical fact] | [plain English outcome for the buyer] |
```

Every product page must include a minimum of 2 differentiators, stated explicitly.

### Objection Map

Before writing any page, list every objection a buyer in this category commonly has. Every objection must be answered somewhere on every product page.

```
| Objection | Answer (outcome first, spec as proof) |
|---|---|
| [objection] | [answer] |
| [objection] | [answer] |
```

Standard objections for most product categories:
- Is this safe to use?
- Will it work for my specific situation/size/weight/environment?
- Will it last?
- Is it difficult to set up or install?
- What happens if something goes wrong?
- Can I control or manage it remotely?
- Is this worth the price?
- What makes this better than [competitor]?

## PART 3 — BUYER PSYCHOLOGY

Understanding why buyers make decisions is the foundation of all effective copy. Write to the driver, not the feature.

### The Five Buyer Drivers

Every purchase decision is driven by one or more of these motivations. Identify which are primary for your audience before writing.

**1. Safety**
The buyer is worried something will go wrong — for themselves, their family, their property, or their business. Copy must address the risk directly, then eliminate it with a specific, verifiable answer.

**2. Convenience**
The buyer wants their life to be easier. Copy must show exactly how the product removes friction — specific actions the buyer no longer has to take, or problems they no longer have to manage.

**3. Status**
The buyer wants to feel that their choice reflects well on them — their property looks better, their business runs more professionally, their home is more modern. Copy must describe the visible, experiential outcome, not just the product capability.

**4. Durability**
The buyer has been burned before. They bought something that failed. Copy must back longevity claims with exact cycle counts, warranty terms, material ratings, or independent test data.

**5. ROI / Value**
The buyer is justifying the spend — to themselves or others. Copy must make the value calculation obvious: what does this cost vs. what does it replace, prevent, or enable?

### Buyer Segment Table Template

Fill in for every project:

```
| Segment | Primary pain point | Primary driver | Messaging angle |
|---|---|---|---|
| [segment] | [pain] | [driver] | [how to frame the copy] |
| [segment] | [pain] | [driver] | [how to frame the copy] |
```

### The Buyer's Natural Question Sequence

When a buyer lands on a product page, they ask questions in a predictable order. The page must answer them in this sequence — not the order that feels logical to the seller.

1. What is this? (category identification)
2. Is this for someone like me? (relevance check)
3. Does it solve my specific problem? (fit check)
4. Can I trust that it actually works? (proof check)
5. What could go wrong? (objection check)
6. Is this better than the alternatives? (differentiation check)
7. What do I do next? (CTA)

If the page answers these out of order, the reader loses confidence and leaves. Audit every page against this sequence before finalising.

## PART 4 — COPYWRITING STYLE: THE NICE METHOD

All copy in this system follows the NICE Method — derived from analysis of high-performing product marketing pages. The method applies to every section of every page without exception.

### The Proof Formula

Every claim follows this structure:

```
OUTCOME HEADLINE (5–8 words)
One sentence connecting the experience to the proof — with the number embedded naturally.
```

**The outcome earns attention. The sentence earns belief.**

The headline states what the buyer gains. The sentence proves it with a specific number embedded naturally — not listed separately as a label or badge.

**Example (gate automation):**
> Silent Every Time.
> Every motor in the range runs at or below 60 dB — quieter than a normal conversation.

**Example (any product category):**
> Works Through Any Winter.
> Tested from -35°C to 70°C — the same unit performs without modification in a Canadian winter or an Australian summer.

**The number must always live inside a sentence that a person would naturally say.** Never float a number alone. Numbers without context do not build trust — they create questions.

---

### Headline Rules

- State what the buyer **experiences or gains** — never what the product **has**
- 5–8 words for section headlines
- 3–4 words for card or feature labels
- No adjective without a number behind it — "powerful" never appears alone
- Every headline must pass the 3-second test: a reader skimming the page stops here

**Failing headlines:**
- "Our Motor Technology" — describes the section, does not sell it
- "Product Features" — generic, no outcome
- "Advanced Control System" — adjective without proof

**Passing headlines:**
- "Silent at Every Speed." — outcome (no noise)
- "Your Gate. Your Phone. Anywhere." — outcome (remote control)
- "Handles Gates Most Openers Refuse." — outcome (heavy load capacity)

---

### Sentence Rules

- One idea per sentence — never combine two ideas with "and" when two sentences are stronger
- Maximum 20 words per sentence in body copy
- Active voice only — no passive constructions
- "You" appears more than "we" in every section
- Mix sentence lengths: short (5–10 words) for punch, medium (11–20 words) for explanation
- Never write three long sentences in a row
- No two consecutive sentences may start with the same word

---

### Proof Rules

- Every benefit claim needs one specific number behind it
- Numbers are embedded naturally in sentences — never floated as standalone labels
- Numbers are exact — not rounded unless the source rounds them
- Proof comes after the outcome, never before
- Every number must be traceable to a verified source document

**Specificity Ladder — every claim must reach Level 3 minimum:**

```
Level 1 (fails): "Handles heavy loads"
Level 2 (fails): "Handles very heavy loads"
Level 3 (passes): "Handles loads up to 1,800 kg"
Level 4 (target): "Handles loads up to 1,800 kg — the full weight range of residential and light commercial gates"
```

---

### Banned Words and Phrases

These words and phrases are banned from all copy. They carry no information, signal AI-generated text, and erode reader trust. No exceptions.

**Filler adjectives:**
cutting-edge · state-of-the-art · world-class · next-generation · innovative · revolutionary · game-changing · industry-leading · advanced (alone) · powerful (alone) · high quality · premium (alone) · robust · seamless · seamlessly

**Corporate verbs:**
leverage · elevate · empower · revolutionize · transform · optimize · streamline · unlock · harness · pioneer

**Vague descriptors:**
reliable (without a spec number) · durable (without a cycle count) · versatile · flexible · comprehensive · holistic · end-to-end · tailor-made · bespoke

**Opening clichés:**
In today's world · In the modern era · In an ever-changing landscape · In today's fast-paced · As technology evolves · In the digital age

**Soft openers:**
Whether you're looking for · Look no further · If you're searching for · The good news is · The fact is

**False authority:**
Our team of experts · Dedicated team · Passionate professionals · Years of experience (without a number) · Trusted by thousands (without a verified count)

**Weak CTAs:**
Learn more · Click here · Find out more · Discover more · Read more · See more · Explore our range

**Filler phrases:**
Don't hesitate to contact us · Feel free to reach out · We would love to hear from you

**Brand chest-beating:**
At [Company], we believe · We are proud to · We are committed to · Our mission is to

**Emotional manipulation:**
Peace of mind (replace with a specific outcome) · Don't miss out · Act now · Limited time · Only X left

---

### CTA Rules

Every call to action must tell the reader exactly what they get when they click it.

**Format that passes:** [Action verb] + [specific thing the reader receives]

**Three-part test for every CTA:**
1. **Substitution test** — replace the CTA with "Click here." If it means the same thing, the CTA fails.
2. **Value exchange test** — does the CTA state what the reader receives? If not, it fails.
3. **Section alignment test** — does the CTA match what the section was about? If not, it fails.

**Passing CTAs:**
- "Download the full specification sheet"
- "Compare all [X] models side by side"
- "Get a quote for your [specific situation]"
- "Find the right [product] for your [use case]"

**Failing CTAs:**
- "Learn more" — fails substitution test
- "Contact us" — acceptable only in contact sections
- "Explore our range" — fails value exchange test

**One CTA per section.** Two CTAs create decision paralysis.

---

### Section Structure (NICE Method)

```
Eyebrow — 2–3 word category label, ALL CAPS
H2 — outcome headline, 5–8 words
Subtext — one sentence of context, 15–20 words maximum
Content — cards / body copy / table
CTA — one specific action
```

### Product Card Structure (NICE Method)

```
One-line positioning statement — what makes this model the right choice
Key outcome sentence — what it does for the buyer, with spec number embedded naturally
CTA — specific action
```

## PART 5 — COPYWRITING FRAMEWORKS

Choose the framework based on the section type. Apply it as a structural guide — not a template to fill in.

### PAS — Problem → Agitate → Solution

**Use for:** FAQ sections, objection handling, technology explanation sections

```
Problem: [Name the problem the buyer has — in their words, not yours]
Agitate: [Explain why the problem is worse than they realised — a hidden risk or cost]
Solution: [State how the product eliminates the problem — with a specific number as proof]
```

**Example:**
- Problem: Manual gates are a security gap. Anyone can push them open.
- Agitate: And most electric gates run on AC power — which adds an electric shock risk your family may not know about.
- Solution: 24V DC eliminates that risk at the source. Safe to touch. Even mid-cycle.

---

### FAB — Feature → Advantage → Benefit

**Use for:** Feature sections, product highlight cards, why-choose-us sections

```
Feature: What the product has [technical fact]
Advantage: What that feature does differently or better [functional result]
Benefit: What the buyer experiences as a result [personal outcome]
```

The copy must lead with the Benefit. Feature and Advantage are supporting evidence.

**The "So what?" method:**
After writing any sentence, ask "So what?" If the answer reveals information not yet in the sentence, add it.

Keep asking until you reach the real buyer outcome. That outcome is the sentence lead.

**The "which means that" bridge:**
Connect a feature to its benefit: "[Feature] — which means that [benefit]."
Then rewrite benefit-first, dropping the bridge phrase.

**Example:**
- Feature: DC24V servo motor
- Advantage: Runs below the electric shock threshold
- Benefit: Safe to touch — for children, pets, and anyone near the gate

Written benefit-first: "Safe to touch — even mid-cycle. The DC24V motor runs well below the voltage where electric shock becomes a risk."

---

### AIDA — Attention → Interest → Desire → Action

**Use for:** Hero sections, product page introductions, landing page openers

```
Attention: Outcome headline — what the buyer gains
Interest: One-sentence proof with spec number embedded
Desire: Key benefit + use case image or example
Action: Specific CTA
```

---

### StoryBrand

**Use for:** Overall page narrative structure, about pages, brand story sections

```
Customer is the hero — they have a problem to solve
Product is the tool — it gives them the capability to solve it
Brand is the guide — knowledgeable, trustworthy, not the star of the story
```

The page should feel like it is told from the customer's perspective, not the brand's. Every section asks: what does the customer gain here? Not: what does the brand want to say?

## PART 6 — THE 7-STEP WRITING WORKFLOW

Follow this workflow for every page, every time. No steps may be skipped or reordered.

---

### Step 0 — Page Brief

Before any outline. Before any writing.

Produce this brief and present it to the client for approval:

```
PAGE BRIEF — [Page Name]

Target buyer: [specific segment from the buyer table — one primary]
Primary keyword: [exact phrase from keyword research]
Secondary keywords: [list]

Keyword slot assignment:
- H1: [primary keyword — exact match]
- First sentence of opening paragraph: [primary keyword]
- Mid-page H2: [primary keyword or close secondary]
- Body paragraph: [primary keyword — different section from H2]
- Image alt text: [primary keyword in context]
- CTA section: [primary keyword]
- Meta description: [primary keyword in first 10 words]

Secondary keywords placed in:
- [section name]: [keyword]
- [section name]: [keyword]

Draft meta title: [Primary Keyword] | [Brand] — [character count]/60
Draft meta description: [benefit-led, primary keyword first] — [character count]/155

Key specs to feature (from verified source):
- [spec with exact number]
- [spec with exact number]

Differentiators to include (minimum 2):
- [ ] [differentiator 1 in consumer language]
- [ ] [differentiator 2 in consumer language]

Objections this page must answer:
- [ ] [objection 1]
- [ ] [objection 2]
- [ ] [objection 3]
[full list from objection map]
```

**Wait for client approval before proceeding.**

---

### Step 1 — Outline with Keyword Map

After the brief is approved, produce the full page outline. Three columns required:

```
| # | Section / Component | Purpose | Keyword slot |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | [component name] | [what it says in one line] | Primary keyword — H1 + first sentence |
| 2 | [component name] | [what it says in one line] | None |
| 3 | [component name] | [what it says in one line] | Secondary keyword: [keyword] |
```

The keyword slot column locks where every keyword lands before writing begins. No surprises during writing.

**Wait for client approval. If the outline changes, update the keyword map and confirm again.**

---

### Step 2 — Write Section by Section

For every section, follow this exact sequence:

**2a — State the component name**

**2b — Show how it displays**
```
DISPLAYS AS:
Layout: [full-width / two columns / card grid / etc.]
Left side: [content left or top]
Right side: [content right or bottom]
User sees: [one sentence — what the visitor notices first]
```

**2c — Write the content**
- NICE proof formula — outcome headline first, spec number in sentence
- Benefit-first every sentence
- Active voice, max 20 words per sentence
- Consumer language — no unexplained technical terms

**2d — Run Tier 1 quality checks** (all five — before showing the client)

See Part 7 of this document for full execution method.

- Check 1: Banned phrase scan
- Check 2: Claims verification
- Check 3: Consumer readability
- Check 4: Benefit-first test
- Check 5: CTA specificity test (if section has a CTA)

**2e — Run keyword check**
```
KEYWORD CHECK — [Section name]
Primary keyword slot: [which slot / confirmed present / not yet placed]
Secondary keywords present: [list]
Note: [flag if any keyword feels forced]
```

**2f — Present to client for approval**
Show: component name → DISPLAYS AS → content → keyword check.
Wait for approval before moving to the next section.

---

### Step 3 — Consumer Journey Read

After all sections are written and approved. Before any pass.

Read the compiled page end-to-end as the target buyer. Check four things:

1. **Question order** — Does the page answer buyer questions in their natural sequence? (See Part 3 — Buyer's Natural Question Sequence)

2. **Flow** — Any section that feels disconnected, too long, or off-topic? Fix before proceeding.

3. **CTA readiness** — Does the reader have enough information to act on the CTA by the time they reach it? If not, move the CTA.

4. **Missing objections** — Cross-check the page against the objection map in the brief. Every objection must be answered. Add answers to existing sections — do not create new sections for individual objections.

Fix everything here before the passes run. No document output — only structural fixes.

---

### Step 4 — Three-Pass System

Run immediately after Step 3. No client approval between passes. Present all three outputs together.

See Part 7 of this document for full execution method of each pass.

**Pass A — Full-page quality audit**
Score six dimensions using the failure count method. Produce annotated failure list.

**Pass B — Surgical rewrite**
Fix every item on the Pass A failure list. Only those items. Nothing else.

**Pass C — Conversion review**
Six consumer-perspective tests. Assess AI-written score. Target: 15% or below.
If above 15%: rewrite top 3 AI-signal sentences, reassess. Maximum 2 loops.

---

### Step 5 — Differentiation Check

One question: Does this page make it clear — in plain language — why a buyer should choose this brand over a competitor they are also looking at?

Minimum 2 differentiators from the client's differentiator reference card must appear on the page, stated explicitly in buyer language.

If fewer than 2 are present: add to the most relevant existing section. One sentence minimum. Do not create a new section.

---

## PART 7 — QUALITY CHECK SYSTEM

> This is the execution guide. Every check has a specific test method — not a general impression.
> "Feels good" is not a pass condition. Each check produces a binary result: pass or fail.

---

### TIER 1 — Section-Level Checks

Run on every section immediately after writing. Before showing the client.

---

#### Check 1 — Banned Phrase Scan

**Method:** Read the section word by word against the full banned list in Part 4. Do not skim.

**Pass condition:** Zero matches.

**Fail condition:** Any match found.

**Rule on failure:** Do not adjust the banned phrase — remove or rewrite the entire sentence. The replacement must carry a real number or a real outcome. The banned word is not the problem — the absence of information behind it is.

---

#### Check 2 — Claims Verification

**Method:** Go through the section sentence by sentence. For every factual claim:

1. **Is there a number?** Find that exact number in the verified source document. If not there, remove the number and the claim.
2. **Is there a product capability claim?** (works in X conditions, compatible with Y, rated for Z) Trace it to the source. If not confirmed, remove it.
3. **Is there a company claim?** (factory-direct, ships complete, X units per year) Trace it. If not confirmed, remove it.

**Apply the Specificity Ladder:**
- Level 1: "Handles heavy loads" — fails
- Level 2: "Handles very heavy loads" — fails
- Level 3: "Handles loads up to 1,800 kg" — passes
- Level 4: "Handles loads up to 1,800 kg — the full weight range of residential and light commercial applications" — target

Every verified claim must reach Level 3 minimum.

**Pass condition:** Every claim traced to source. Every performance claim at Level 3 or higher.

**Fail condition:** Any unverified claim or any claim at Level 1 or 2.

---

#### Check 3 — Consumer Readability

Run four sub-tests. All must pass.

**Test A — Stranger test**
Read the section as if explaining it to someone who has never encountered this product category. Every word or phrase you would need to explain in conversation is a failure. Mark it.

Technical terms are allowed only if explained in the same sentence.
Format: "[Technical term] — [plain English explanation]"

**Test B — Sentence length**
Count words in every sentence. Flag anything over 25 words. Split at the earliest natural break.

Target distribution:
- 30% short sentences: 5–10 words
- 50% medium sentences: 11–20 words
- 20% longer sentences: 21–25 words
- 0% sentences over 25 words

**Test C — Passive voice**
Identify every sentence where the subject receives the action rather than performs it.
Every passive construction must be rewritten active.

Passive: "Obstacle detection is included as standard."
Active: "Obstacle detection stops the gate the instant it contacts anything in its path."

**Test D — Jargon ratio**
Count technical terms in the section. Every one must be explained inline. The ratio of unexplained technical terms must be zero.

**Pass condition:** All four tests pass.

---

#### Check 4 — Benefit-First Test

**Method — FAB chain:**
For every sentence describing a product feature, identify:
- Feature: what the product has
- Advantage: what that does differently
- Benefit: what the buyer experiences

The sentence must open with the Benefit.

**Method — "So what?" test:**
Read the sentence. Ask "So what?" If the answer reveals important information not in the sentence, add it. Keep asking until you reach the real buyer outcome. That outcome leads the sentence.

**Method — "Which means that" bridge:**
"[Feature] — which means that [benefit]."
Rewrite benefit-first. Drop the bridge phrase.

**Pass condition:** Every sentence that describes a product feature opens with the buyer outcome.

**Fail condition:** Any sentence opens with a product attribute before the buyer benefit.

---

#### Check 5 — CTA Specificity Test

**Only for sections that contain a CTA.**

**Part 1 — Substitution test:** Replace the CTA with "Click here." If the meaning does not change, it fails.

**Part 2 — Value exchange test:** Does the CTA state what the reader receives when they click? If not, it fails.
Required format: [Action verb] + [specific thing the reader gets]

**Part 3 — Section alignment test:** Does the CTA match the section topic? If not, it fails.

**Pass condition:** All three parts pass.

**One CTA per section maximum.**

---

### TIER 2 — Three-Pass System

Run after the full page is compiled. The section checks above mean the page arriving here is already clean. The three passes refine a good page — they do not rescue a bad one.

---

#### Pass A — Full-Page Quality Audit

**Method:**
Read the full page once. Mark every sentence that fails any of the five Tier 1 checks. Then score each dimension using the failure count method.

**Scoring:**

| Failures found | Score |
|---|---|
| 0 | 10/10 |
| 1 | 9/10 |
| 2–3 | 7–8/10 |
| 4–6 | 5–6/10 |
| 7–10 | 3–4/10 |
| 11+ | 1–2/10 |

**Six dimensions:**

**Clarity** — Count: unexplained technical terms + sentences over 25 words

**Specificity** — Count: unverified claims + Level 1 or Level 2 claims

**Human Voice** — Count: banned phrases + consecutive same-word sentence starts + sentences that read as assembled from parts

The "assembled" test: Read the sentence aloud. Signs of assembled writing:
- Subject → verb → object with no variation, repeated
- Three or more sentences in a row following the same grammatical structure
- Vocabulary that is technically correct but not how a person would naturally say it
- Perfect parallelism in bullet points (every bullet same length and structure)

**Consumer Fit** — Count: feature-first sentences + sections with unexplained industry assumptions

**Benefit-First** — Count: sentences where product feature appears before buyer benefit

**Originality** — Count: repeated phrases across the page + structural repetition clusters (3+ sentences in same pattern)

**Output format:**
```
PASS A AUDIT — [Page Name]
Clarity: [X]/10 — [main failure type found]
Specificity: [X]/10 — [main failure type found]
Human Voice: [X]/10 — [main failure type found]
Consumer Fit: [X]/10 — [main failure type found]
Benefit-First: [X]/10 — [main failure type found]
Originality: [X]/10 — [main failure type found]
Overall: [average]/10

Failure list:
- [Section]: [exact sentence] — [which check failed] — [fix instruction]
```

Every failure list item must name the exact sentence, the check it failed, and the direction of the fix. "Rewrite this section" is not an acceptable instruction. Be specific.

---

#### Pass B — Surgical Rewrite

**Method:**
Work through the Pass A failure list item by item. Fix only what failed. Do not rewrite passing sentences.

**Eight rewriting rules — apply to every fix:**

**Rule 1 — Sentence start variation**
No two consecutive sentences may start with the same word. After fixing a sentence, check the one before and after it.

**Rule 2 — Number grounding**
Every vague claim gets a specific verified number. Use the source document. Do not estimate.
"Long-lasting" → "rated for [X] operating cycles"
"Works in all climates" → "operates from [X]°C to [X]°C"

**Rule 3 — Sentence length discipline**
After rewriting, count the words. Over 25: split it. Contains two ideas joined by "and" or "but": test whether two sentences are stronger. Usually they are.

**Rule 4 — Active voice**
Find the agent of the action. Make it the subject of the sentence.

**Rule 5 — One idea per sentence**
If a sentence contains a comma separating two independent clauses, test whether each works better alone.

**Rule 6 — Sentence rhythm**
After completing fixes in a section, read it aloud. Count the beats. Short sentences punch. Long sentences explain. Vary them. If three or more sentences feel the same length, vary one.

**Rule 7 — Buyer language**
Write in the words buyers use when they search and speak — not the words the product engineer uses.

**Rule 8 — No urgency language**
Do not use urgency triggers. Buyers in most considered-purchase categories research over days or weeks. Urgency language reads as manipulation and damages trust.

**Output format:**
```
PASS B COMPLETE — [Page Name]
Items fixed: [count]
Sections touched: [list]
Items needing client data to resolve: [list — remove the claim, note the data needed]
```

---

#### Pass C — Consumer Conversion Review

**Method:**
Read the page as the target buyer — someone who has never heard of this brand, is comparing options, and will leave if they are not convinced within 90 seconds.

**Six tests:**

**Test 1 — 3-second headline test**
Read only each section's headline for 3 seconds. Does it make a promise or state an outcome the target buyer wants to know more about?

Failing headlines: describe the section, name the product, list the company, use adjectives without proof.
Passing headlines: state an outcome, make a promise, raise a relevant question the next sentence answers.

Every failing headline must be rewritten as an outcome statement of 5–8 words.

**Test 2 — First 10 words test**
Read only the first 10 words of each section's body copy. Do they state what the buyer gains?

If the first 10 words introduce the product, company, or feature — they fail.
The first 10 words must introduce what the buyer receives.

**Test 3 — Objection coverage test**
Cross-check the full page against the objection map in the brief. Every objection must be answered with a specific, number-backed response.

List uncovered objections. Add answers to the most relevant existing sections before proceeding.

**Test 4 — CTA journey test**
Map every CTA on the page. Does each one advance the reader one step closer to a decision or conversion?

The CTA journey should follow a logical progression:
- Early page: orientation CTA (find the right product, compare options)
- Mid page: verification CTA (download spec, see all features)
- End of page: conversion CTA (get a quote, contact, order)

No two CTAs should send the reader to the same destination. If they do, remove one.

**Test 5 — Differentiation test**
Read the page as a buyer who has also looked at a competitor's page. What does this page tell them that the competitor's page does not?

Minimum 2 differentiators from the client's differentiator reference card must appear, stated explicitly in buyer language. If fewer than 2 are present, add to an existing section.

**Test 6 — Read-aloud test**
Read the full page aloud from start to finish. Mark every moment where:
- You hesitate because the sentence does not flow
- You read a sentence twice because the meaning is unclear on first pass
- A phrase sounds like it was written, not spoken

Every marked sentence is a rewrite.

---

#### AI-Written Score

The AI-written score is an informed reader's assessment of how "generated" the copy feels. Target: 15% or below.

**Signals that raise the score (+5% each):**
- Three or more consecutive sentences in the same grammatical structure
- "not only... but also" construction
- Transitions: Furthermore · Additionally · Moreover · In conclusion · It is worth noting
- Perfect parallel bullet points (every bullet same length and structure)
- Any CTA that could apply to any product on any website
- Vocabulary that is technically correct but no person would naturally use it

**Signals that lower the score (-5% each):**
- Intentional sentence fragment used for punch
- Sentence starting with "And" or "But"
- A specific non-round number (e.g. 1,250 not 1,200)
- Direct mid-paragraph address to the reader
- An unexpected analogy grounding an abstract claim
- A rhetorical question answered immediately by the next sentence

**If above 15%:**
Identify the top 3 AI-signal sentences. Rewrite them. Reassess.
Maximum 2 loops. If still above 15% after 2 loops, flag the remaining sentences to the client with a specific note on what is driving the score.

## PART 8 — SEO RULES

### Keyword Placement

Every page requires 6–7 placements of the primary keyword across these specific slots:

| Slot | Location | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | H1 | Exact match — no variations, no creative rewording |
| 2 | First sentence, opening paragraph | Primary keyword in first 10 words |
| 3 | One mid-page H2 | Exact match or close secondary |
| 4 | One body paragraph | Not the same section as slot 3 |
| 5 | At least one image alt text | Keyword in natural context |
| 6 | CTA section body copy | Embedded naturally in the sentence |
| 7 | Meta description | Primary keyword in first 10 words |

**Rules:**
- Never use the primary keyword twice in the same paragraph
- Never force it into a sentence where it reads unnaturally — rewrite the sentence
- Use exact-match keyword phrases from the keyword research — do not paraphrase
- Secondary keywords fill remaining body naturally — no target count, just relevance

### Meta Tags

- **Meta title:** `[Primary Keyword] | [Brand Name]` — maximum 60 characters
- **Meta description:** Benefit-led sentence, primary keyword in first 10 words — maximum 155 characters
- Every page needs a unique meta title and meta description — never duplicate

### Internal Links

- Every page links to at least 2 other pages
- Anchor text must use the exact target keyword of the destination page
- Never use "click here", "learn more", "this page", or "here" as anchor text
- Follow the site's SILO structure — only link within correct tier paths

### SILO Structure Principles

A keyword silo organises pages into topic clusters. Pages within a cluster reinforce each other's keyword authority. Pages outside the cluster do not link to each other.

**Core rule:** Pages only link to their parent tier or their direct children.

**Standard three-tier structure:**
- Tier 1: Homepage → links down to all Tier 2 pages
- Tier 2: Category pages → links up to Tier 1, down to Tier 3 children
- Tier 3: Product/detail pages → links up to Tier 2 and Tier 1

**Anchor text rule:** The anchor text linking to a page must use that page's exact primary keyword.

## PART 11 — COMMON FAILURE PATTERNS

These are the most frequently found failures across product copy. Check for these specifically on every page.

| Pattern | Example of failure | Correct approach |
|---|---|---|
| Feature lead | "The operator includes a DC24V motor" | "Safe to touch — the 24V motor runs below the shock threshold" |
| Vague performance claim | "Handles heavy loads" | "Handles loads up to [X] kg" (verify from source) |
| Unanchored condition claim | "Works in all climates" | "Operates from [X]°C to [X]°C" |
| Safety claim without proof | "Safe for families" | "Safe to touch — [voltage] runs well below the electric shock threshold" |
| Noise claim without measurement | "Quiet operation" | "Runs at just [X] dB — quieter than a normal conversation" |
| Speed claim without number | "Fast cycle times" | "Opens a [X]-metre gate in under [X] seconds" |
| Compatibility claim without specifics | "Smart home compatible" | "Works with [platform] — compatible with most smart home systems" |
| Generic CTA | "Contact us to learn more" | "Request a quote for your [specific situation]" |
| Unverified certification claim | "Certified and compliant" | Only claim certifications confirmed in source data |
| Passive safety claim | "Obstacle detection is included" | "Obstacle detection stops the gate the instant it contacts anything" |
| Round number that is not from source | "Up to 2,000 kg capacity" | Use the exact number from the spec document |

## PART 12 — WHAT NOT TO DO

These rules apply to every project without exception.

- Do not invent specifications. Every number must come from a verified source document.
- Do not claim certifications, awards, or compliance unless confirmed in writing from the client.
- Do not write in first person plural ("We offer...", "We believe..."). Write in second person or third person.
- Do not run the three-pass system on individual sections mid-writing. Tier 1 checks run per section. The three passes run on the full compiled page only.
- Do not float spec numbers as standalone labels. Embed them in sentences.
- Do not skip the consumer journey read before running passes. Structural problems are cheaper to fix before passes run.
- Do not rewrite content that has already passed all three passes. Improve by adding — not replacing.
- Do not write two CTAs in the same section.
- Do not use urgency language for considered-purchase products.
- Do not skip Step 0 (Page Brief) or Step 1 (Outline). These steps prevent wasted writing.
- Do not present any section to the client before running all five Tier 1 checks.
