---

**Tile 1 — Sliding or Swing?**

The decision comes down to clearance. Swing gates rotate outward — they need space in front of the opening. Sliding gates travel along the fence line — they need a clear run to one side.

If the driveway slopes, space is tight, or vehicles park close to the gate, sliding is the safer specification. If you have the clearance and want the look of a swinging entrance, swing is the right choice.

Compare sliding gate operator vs swing gate operator →

---

**Tile 2 — How heavy is your gate?**

Gate weight is the most common specification error — and an undersized motor pays for it quickly. Running at or above its rated capacity, the motor runs hot, wears fast, and fails early.

Weigh the gate leaf before specifying. Open Bear sliding operators handle up to 1,800 kg. The heavy-duty swing operator handles up to 1,200 kg with 650 Nm of torque. If the weight is near the limit, size up.

View heavy duty swing gate operator →

---

**Tile 3 — High-traffic or access control?**

For entrances that open hundreds of times a day — car parks, logistics gates, barrier-controlled access points — a standard swing or sliding operator is the wrong tool. The duty cycle will destroy it within months.

The Open Bear barrier gate completes a full trip in 0.7 seconds and is rated for 10 million cycles. It is built specifically for the volume and speed that access control environments demand.

View barrier gate operator →
