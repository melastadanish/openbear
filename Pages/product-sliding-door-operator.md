### [content-how-it-works·var-a]

```
eyebrow: HOW IT WORKS
h2: Four Components. One Gate That Opens Every Time.
subtitle: A sliding gate opener is a system — motor, controller, safety sensors, and power backup working together.

step-1-title: The Motor
step-1-body: Open Bear systems use DC24V servo permanent magnet synchronous motors — not AC asynchronous motors. This eliminates the electric leakage risk common to older gate systems and delivers smoother, more precise motion control.

step-2-title: The Controller
step-2-body: The brain of the system. Ten programmable settings control open speed, close speed, auto-close delay, deceleration position, and obstacle response. Set them once during installation. The controller holds them.

step-3-title: The Safety System
step-3-body: Every sliding gate opener in the Open Bear range supports photocell and safety edge integration. If anything breaks the beam or contacts the safety edge, the gate stops and reverses immediately — protecting children, pets, and vehicles without manual intervention.

step-4-title: Power Backup
step-4-body: When the grid goes down, the gate keeps working. The controller supports battery backup connection — your gate opens and closes normally through a power cut, no manual override needed.
```

### [content-faq·var-a]

```
eyebrow: COMMON QUESTIONS
h2: What People Ask Before Buying a Sliding Gate Opener

q1: Can I control this from my phone?
a1: Two RF remote controls ship with every kit — use them from your car, intercom point, or anywhere on the property. For phone app control, a small plug-in module connects the controller to your home WiFi and lets you trigger the gate from an app. Ask our team which accessory works with this model before you order.

q2: How loud is the motor when it opens?
a2: Both models run at 60 dB or below — roughly the level of a normal conversation at arm's length. You will hear the gate moving from beside it. From inside the house with windows closed, you will not.

q3: How hard is it to install?
a3: The kit arrives complete — motor unit, controller, two remotes, anchor bolts, end stops, and all mounting hardware in one box. Most homeowners use a local gate installer for the mechanical mounting and wiring. An installer sets the 10 controller parameters once during commissioning — opening speed, closing speed, auto-close delay, and obstacle response. The controller stores them — no re-entry needed after a power cycle.

q4: What happens when the power goes out?
a4: The controller supports a battery backup module. With one fitted, the gate opens and closes normally through a power cut — no manual release, no getting locked out.
```
