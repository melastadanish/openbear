COMMON QUESTIONS

**H2:**
Everything You Need to Know Before Choosing an Automatic Gate Opener.

---

**How do I choose the right automatic gate opener for my property?**

Three things determine the right model: gate type, gate weight, and how often it will operate each day.

Gate type comes first. Sliding gates need a sliding operator — the motor drives a rack along the bottom of the gate leaf. Swing gates need a swing operator — the motor pushes or pulls the leaf through a 90-degree arc. Barrier arms need a barrier mechanism. These are not interchangeable.

Gate weight comes second — and this is where most buyers make the wrong call. Open Bear sliding operators handle up to 1,200 kg on the 200W model and up to 1,800 kg on the 400W model. The heavy-duty swing operator handles leaves up to 1,200 kg and 6 metres wide. Lighter swing gates under 200 kg suit the side-mounted, floor, or top-mounted operators.

Duty cycle comes third. A residential driveway gate opening 20 times a day has different requirements from a commercial car park barrier opening 300 times a day. For high-frequency applications, check the life cycle rating. The Open Bear barrier gate is rated for 10 million operating cycles — built for the most demanding access control environments.

---

**What is the difference between AC and DC gate motors — and why does it matter?**

DC motors are safer and smoother. That is the short answer.

AC gate motors run at mains voltage — typically 220V. That voltage is high enough to leak current into a metal gate frame through worn insulation or a wiring fault. In wet conditions, touching that gate frame becomes a genuine electric shock hazard.

Open Bear motors run at 24 volts DC — below the threshold that causes electric shock. This is not a safety feature bolted on top. It is a property of operating at low voltage. Even if current leaked into the frame, contact with it would cause no injury.

The performance difference is just as real. DC servo motors respond to the controller's commands precisely — they accelerate on a programmable curve, decelerate to a set position, and stop exactly where instructed. The result is smoother gate movement, less mechanical stress on hinges and hardware over time, and quieter operation at or below 60 dB under load.

---

**How loud is an automatic gate opener during operation?**

Quiet enough to install in a residential street without complaints.

Every Open Bear gate operator runs at or below 60 dB during operation — roughly the volume of a normal conversation. The heavy-duty swing operator, which drives the largest gate leaves in the range, runs at or below 65 dB.

Two design choices keep the noise down. DC servo motors do not carry the electromagnetic hum of AC motors. And the servo controller applies soft-start and soft-stop to every cycle — the gate accelerates gradually and decelerates smoothly before reaching the end of travel. No jolt. No clunk. No noise spike at the end of each open or close.

---

**Are automatic gate openers safe for children and pets?**

Yes — with the right safety setup, an automatic gate is one of the safest ways to secure an entrance.

Open Bear controllers include obstacle detection as standard. If the gate meets unexpected resistance during travel — a child, a pet, a bicycle — the controller detects the increased motor load and immediately stops and reverses. The sensitivity is adjustable via the controller's P-series parameters, set by the installer to match the gate weight and site conditions.

For complete protection, pair the operator with photocell safety sensors. These project an infrared beam across the gate opening. If anything breaks the beam while the gate is closing, the gate stops and reverses before making contact. Photocells are the industry-standard safety addition for any gate with automatic closing and are recommended for all residential installations with children or pets.

---

**What happens to my gate if the power goes out?**

The gate keeps working — if you have battery backup fitted.

All Open Bear operators are compatible with battery backup modules. When mains power is interrupted, the battery takes over automatically with no delay and no manual action required. The gate opens and closes exactly as normal.

Without battery backup, every Open Bear operator includes a manual release. On the heavy-duty swing operator, this is the orange button on the motor housing. Manual release disconnects the motor from the gate mechanism so the gate can be pushed open or closed by hand, regardless of whether power is available.

For properties in regions with frequent outages, battery backup is a standard specification rather than an optional extra. Open Bear DC24V motors draw low current compared to AC alternatives — they place less demand on battery capacity and operate longer between charges.

---

**Can I control my automatic gate opener from my phone?**

Yes. The Open Bear Side-Mounted Swing Operator has Bluetooth and WiFi built directly into the unit — open, close, and monitor your gate from a smartphone without any additional hardware.

Every other model in the range connects to third-party smart access controllers via the auxiliary terminals on the gate controller. These systems handle the phone communication independently of the motor — which means any Open Bear operator can be part of a smart access setup. The motor and the connectivity are separate, and both are fully supported.

For properties requiring video intercom, RFID access, or vehicle number plate recognition, these systems connect to the same auxiliary terminals and are compatible across the full Open Bear range.

---

**Will an automatic gate opener work in extreme cold or heat?**

Open Bear sliding gate and heavy-duty swing operators are rated from -35°C to 70°C — a 105-degree range that covers every major market climate without modification.

That means the same unit ships to Canada, Russia, and Scandinavia at the cold extreme, and to the Middle East and Southeast Asia at the hot extreme. No regional variants. No cold-weather kit. The standard product handles both.

The lighter swing operators — side-mounted, floor, and top-mounted models — are rated from -25°C to 70°C, which covers the majority of temperate and tropical climates globally.

Two things make that range possible. DC servo motors generate less heat than AC motors under equivalent load. And Open Bear motors are rated to a temperature rise of no more than 15°C above ambient under full load — keeping winding temperatures well below the F-class thermal protection threshold of 155°C.

---

**How long does an automatic gate opener last?**

Decades — if the gate is correctly specified and the operator is not being asked to move a gate heavier than its rated capacity.

Open Bear operators are rated by life cycle count. The barrier gate mechanism is rated for a minimum of 10 million operating cycles, with a mean cycles between failures rating of 2 million. At 100 cycles per day that is over 270 years of rated lifespan on the life cycle figure. For residential driveways averaging 20 to 30 cycles per day, any Open Bear operator represents decades of service under normal conditions.

Motor longevity comes from running cool. DC servo motors generate less heat than AC induction motors under load. Open Bear motors rise no more than 15°C above ambient temperature under full load — well below the 155°C winding protection threshold. Low heat means slow insulation degradation, which is the primary mechanism of motor failure over time.

---

**What is the difference between a sliding gate opener and a swing gate opener?**

The gate structure determines the operator type — they are not interchangeable.

A sliding gate opener drives a gate that moves horizontally on a track. The motor engages with a toothed rack on the gate leaf and pulls it sideways. Sliding gates suit driveways where there is not enough room for the gate to swing outward — on a slope, close to a road, or in a confined entrance with parked vehicles nearby.

A swing gate opener drives a gate that rotates on a hinge through an arc of up to 90 degrees. The motor connects to the gate leaf via a swing arm or actuator. Swing gates are the more common residential choice where space allows, and are the standard choice for estate-style entrances where the visual movement of the gate is part of the property's appeal.

Specification works differently for each type. Sliding operators are rated by gate weight — Open Bear handles up to 1,800 kg. Swing operators are rated by torque — Open Bear produces up to 650 Nm, which determines the length and weight of leaf the motor can drive through a full arc.

---

**Can automatic gate openers work with solar power?**

Open Bear operators are well suited to solar installations — better than AC motor alternatives.

AC gate motors require mains-voltage AC power, which means a solar installation needs an inverter to convert the DC output of solar panels to 220V AC. Inverters add cost, complexity, and a point of failure.

Open Bear motors run at 24V DC — the same voltage that solar panels and charge controllers naturally produce. Solar panels charge a 24V battery bank. The battery bank powers the controller and motor directly. No inverter required. The system operates independently of the grid.

This makes Open Bear operators the practical choice for rural properties, farm gates, and any entrance where running mains cabling to the gate post is impractical or expensive. The controller's battery terminals accept input from a standard solar charge controller — no proprietary components are needed.

---

**What maintenance does an automatic gate opener require?**

Less than most people expect — but skipping it shortens the motor's life significantly.

The mechanical components — the rack and pinion on a sliding gate, the swing arm bearings on a swing operator — should be lubricated every six to twelve months. Use a silicone-based lubricant on the rack teeth. Avoid petroleum-based greases, which attract dust and grit and accelerate wear on the drive components.

The gate's physical alignment matters more than most owners realise. A swing gate that has dropped on its hinges, or a sliding gate track with debris in it, forces the motor to work harder on every cycle. Over time that increased load raises motor temperature, accelerates wear on the drive components, and shortens the operator's lifespan. Check and correct gate alignment at the same time as lubrication.

Test the obstacle detection every six months. Push firmly against a closing gate at mid-travel and confirm it stops and reverses immediately. If the response is slow, the obstacle sensitivity parameter on the controller needs recalibration. A gate that does not reverse promptly is a safety hazard — not a maintenance inconvenience.

Where battery backup is fitted, disconnect mains power once a year and confirm the gate operates correctly on battery alone.

---

**Does an automatic gate opener come with everything needed for installation?**

Yes. Every Open Bear gate opener ships as a complete kit — motor unit, digital controller, two remote controls, mounting hardware, anchor bolts, and all fixings for a standard installation. Nothing needs to be sourced separately.

The controller ships with default parameter settings that work for most gate weights and sizes. The installer adjusts the 10 programmable parameters on-site — open speed, close speed, deceleration position, auto-close timing, obstacle sensitivity, and more — to match the specific gate and site.

Photocell safety sensors, keypads, intercom systems, and battery backup modules are available as compatible accessories. All connect to the controller's auxiliary terminals and are supported across the full Open Bear range.
