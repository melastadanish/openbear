## 2. Buyer Psychology

| Driver | What they need to hear |

| Convenience | Phone, remote, keypad. Opens before you reach it. Ships complete — nothing to source separately. |
| Status | Clean install, modern aesthetic, no visible hardware (floor model). |
| Durability | Exact cycle counts. Extreme climate ratings. F-class thermal protection. |
| ROI / Value | Factory-direct — no reseller markup. Full documentation in one box. |

### Buyer Segments
| Segment | Pain Point | Messaging Angle |
|---|---|---|
| Homeowners | Safety, convenience, curb appeal | Silent, safe, controlled from anywhere |
| Property owners | Family safety, noise, phone control | DC24V safe voltage, quiet motor, app-compatible |
| Property managers | Vehicle flow, access, reliability | Heavy-duty performance, one supplier for all gate types |

### Buyer's Question Sequence (page order must follow this)
1. What is this?
2. Is this for someone like me?
3. Does it solve my specific problem?
4. Can I trust it actually works?
5. What could go wrong?
6. Why this over alternatives?
7. What do I do next?

## 3. NICE Copywriting Method

### The Proof Formula
Every claim follows this structure:

```
OUTCOME HEADLINE (5–8 words)
One sentence connecting the experience to the proof — number embedded naturally.
```

> Silent Every Time.
> Every motor in the range runs at or below 60 dB — quieter than a normal conversation.

**The outcome earns attention. The sentence earns belief.**
Numbers live inside sentences. Never floated alone as labels or badges.

### Headline Rules
- State what the buyer **experiences or gains** — never what the product **has**
- 5–8 words for section headlines · 3–4 words for card/feature labels
- No adjective without a number behind it — "powerful" never appears alone
- Must pass the 3-second test: a reader skimming stops here

### Sentence Rules
- One idea per sentence
- Maximum 20 words per sentence in body copy
- Active voice only — no passive constructions
- "You" appears more than "we" in every section
- Mix sentence lengths: short (5–10 words) for punch, medium (11–20 words) for explanation
- No two consecutive sentences may start with the same word

### CTA Rules
Every CTA tells the reader exactly what they get when they click.

**Format:** [Action verb] + [specific thing the reader receives]

✅ "Download the full specification sheet"
✅ "Compare all six models side by side"
✅ "Get a quote for your gate size"
❌ "Learn more" · "Click here" · "Find out more" · "Explore our range"

One CTA per section maximum.

### Banned Words and Phrases
| Category | Banned |
|---|---|
| Filler adjectives | cutting-edge · state-of-the-art · world-class · next-generation · innovative · revolutionary · game-changing · industry-leading · advanced (alone) · powerful (alone) · high quality · premium (alone) · robust · seamless · seamlessly |
| Corporate verbs | leverage · elevate · empower · revolutionize · transform · optimize · streamline · unlock · harness · pioneer |
| Vague descriptors | reliable (without spec number) · durable (without cycle count) · versatile · flexible · comprehensive · holistic · end-to-end · tailor-made · bespoke |
| Opening clichés | In today's world · In the modern era · In an ever-changing landscape · As technology evolves |
| Soft openers | Whether you're looking for · Look no further · If you're searching for · The good news is |
| False authority | Our team of experts · Dedicated team · Passionate professionals · Years of experience (without a number) |
| Weak CTAs | Learn more · Click here · Find out more · Discover more · See more · Explore our range |
| Chest-beating | At Open Bear, we believe · We are proud to · We are committed to · Our mission is to |
| Manipulation | Peace of mind (replace with specific outcome) · Don't miss out · Act now · Limited time |

## 4. Copywriting Frameworks

### FAB — Feature → Advantage → Benefit
Use for: Feature sections, product highlights, why-choose-us cards

The copy must lead with the Benefit. Feature supports it.

**"So what?" method:** After every sentence ask "So what?" If the answer reveals important information not in the sentence, add it. Keep asking until you reach the real buyer outcome. That outcome leads the sentence.

**"Which means that" bridge:** "[Feature] — which means that [benefit]." Then rewrite benefit-first, dropping the bridge.

Example:
- Feature: DC24V servo motor
- Benefit-first: "Safe to touch — even mid-cycle. The DC24V motor runs well below the voltage where electric shock becomes a risk."

### PAS — Problem → Agitate → Solution
Use for: FAQ answers, objection handling, technology sections

- Problem: Manual gates are a security gap.
- Agitate: AC motors add a shock risk your family may not know about.
- Solution: 24V DC eliminates that risk at the source. Safe to touch. Even mid-cycle.

### AIDA — Attention → Interest → Desire → Action
Use for: Hero sections, product page introductions

- Attention: Outcome headline
- Interest: One sentence with spec number embedded
- Desire: Key benefit + use case image
- Action: Specific CTA

### StoryBrand
Use for: Overall page narrative, about sections

- Customer is the hero — they have a problem
- Product is the tool — it solves it
- Brand is the guide — knowledgeable, not the star of the story

## 5. Objection Handling

Every response follows the proof formula — outcome first, spec number embedded.

| Objection | Response |
|---|---|
| "Will someone get an electric shock?" | Safe to touch — even mid-cycle. The motor runs on 24V DC, well below the voltage where electric shock becomes a risk. |
| "Will it work in my weather?" | Tested from -35°C to 70°C. The same unit performs in a Canadian winter and an Australian summer without modification. |
| "What if the power cuts out?" | Battery backup keeps the gate opening and closing normally when the grid goes down. |
| "Is it too loud?" | Runs at just 60 dB — quieter than a normal conversation, at any gate weight. |
| "Will it handle my heavy gate?" | Handles gates up to 1,800 kg with 650 Nm of torque — the weight range covering most residential and commercial gates. |
| "Is it hard to install?" | Ships complete — motor, controller, remotes, and all hardware in one box. An installer has everything they need on arrival. |
| "Can I control it from my phone?" | Bluetooth and WiFi built in on the side-mounted model. Works with most smart home platforms without an additional hub. |
| "Is it safe around children and pets?" | Obstacle detection stops the gate the instant it contacts anything in its path. Combined with 24V DC voltage, safe around people and animals. |

## 6. SEO Rules

### Keyword Placement — 7 slots per page
| Slot | Location | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | H1 | Exact match — no variations |
| 2 | First sentence, opening paragraph | Primary keyword in first 10 words |
| 3 | One mid-page H2 | Exact match or close secondary |
| 4 | One body paragraph | Different section from slot 3 |
| 5 | At least one image alt text | Keyword in natural context |
| 6 | CTA section body copy | Embedded naturally |
| 7 | Meta description | Primary keyword in first 10 words |

- Never use the primary keyword twice in the same paragraph
- Never force it — rewrite the sentence if it reads unnaturally
- Use exact-match phrases from `Reference/Keyword-Clusters/`

### Meta Tags
- **Title:** `[Primary Keyword] | Open Bear` — max 60 characters
- **Description:** Benefit-led, primary keyword in first 10 words — max 155 characters
- Every page must have a unique title and description

### Internal Links
- Every page links to at least 2 other pages
- Anchor text must use the **exact target keyword** of the destination page
- Never use "click here", "learn more", or "here" as anchor text
- Follow SILO tier paths — only link within correct tier

### SILO Rules
- Tier 3 (product pages) → links UP to Tier 2 and Tier 1 only
- Tier 2 (category/archive pages) → links UP to Tier 1, DOWN to Tier 3
- Tier 1 (Homepage) → links DOWN to all Tier 2 pages
- No cross-silo links

## 7. Quality Check System

### Overview
| Tier | When | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Tier 1 — Section checks | After each section | Catch failures early |
| Tier 2 — Three passes | After full page compiled | Refine a clean page |

---

### Tier 1 — Five Section Checks

Run on every section immediately after writing. Before showing the user.

#### Check 1 — Banned Phrase Scan
Read word by word against the banned list in Section 3.
**Pass:** Zero matches. **Fail:** Any match → remove or rewrite the full sentence, replace with a real number or real outcome.

#### Check 2 — Claims Verification
For every factual claim:
1. **Number present?** Find that exact number in `Reference/CLIENT-DATA-MAP.md`. If not there, remove it.
2. **Product capability claim?** Trace to source. If not confirmed, remove it.
3. **Company claim?** Trace to source. If not confirmed, remove it.

**Specificity ladder — every claim must reach Level 3 minimum:**
```
Level 1 (fails): "Handles heavy gates"
Level 2 (fails): "Handles very heavy gates"
Level 3 (passes): "Handles gates up to 1,800 kg"
Level 4 (target): "Handles gates up to 1,800 kg — covering the full range of residential and commercial gate weights"
```

**Special rules:**
- CE certification — confirmed on Side-Mounted Swing only. Do not claim for any other product.
- Founded 2015, gate automation from 2018. Do not use "15+ years" or unverified experience claims.
- Do not use install count figures. Use factory capacity: 50,000 units/year.

#### Check 3 — Consumer Readability (4 sub-tests)
**A — Stranger test:** Read as if explaining to a neighbour with no gate automation knowledge. Every unexplained term is a failure.
- Technical terms allowed only if explained inline: "[Term] — [plain English]"

**B — Sentence length:** Flag any sentence over 25 words. Split at the earliest natural break.
- Target: 30% short (5–10 words) · 50% medium (11–20 words) · 20% long (21–25 words) · 0% over 25

**C — Passive voice:** Identify every sentence where the subject receives the action. Rewrite all active.
- Passive: "Obstacle detection is included as standard."
- Active: "Obstacle detection stops the gate the instant it contacts anything in its path."

**D — Jargon ratio:** Every technical term that appears must be explained in the same sentence. Ratio of unexplained terms: zero.

#### Check 4 — Benefit-First Test
Apply the FAB chain to every sentence that describes a product feature. The sentence must open with the buyer outcome, not the product attribute.

Apply the "So what?" method and the "which means that" bridge (see Section 4).

**Pass:** Every sentence opens with buyer outcome. **Fail:** Any sentence opens with a product attribute first.

#### Check 5 — CTA Specificity Test (CTA sections only)
**Part 1 — Substitution test:** Replace CTA with "Click here." If the meaning does not change, it fails.
**Part 2 — Value exchange test:** Does the CTA state what the reader receives? Required: [Action verb] + [specific thing]
**Part 3 — Section alignment test:** Does the CTA match what the section was about?

**Pass:** All three parts pass. One CTA per section maximum.

---

### Tier 2 — Four-Pass System

> **MANDATORY RULE: All four passes (A, B, C, D) must be run together on every page before content is shown to the user. Never show content after Pass A or Pass C alone. The sequence is: write full page → Pass A → Pass B → Pass D → Pass C → show final content. Do not present intermediate drafts.**

Run after the full page is compiled. The four passes refine a clean page — not rescue a broken one.

#### Pass A — Full-Page Quality Audit
Read the full page once. Mark every sentence that fails any Tier 1 check. Score each of six dimensions using failure count.

**Scoring:** 0 failures = 10/10 · 1 = 9/10 · 2–3 = 7–8/10 · 4–6 = 5–6/10 · 7–10 = 3–4/10 · 11+ = 1–2/10

**Six dimensions:**
- **Clarity** — unexplained jargon + sentences over 25 words
- **Specificity** — unverified claims + Level 1/2 claims
- **Human Voice** — banned phrases + same-word consecutive sentence starts + sentences that read as assembled from parts
- **Consumer Fit** — feature-first sentences + unexplained industry assumptions
- **Benefit-First** — sentences where feature appears before buyer benefit
- **Originality** — repeated phrases + structural repetition clusters (3+ sentences same pattern)

The "assembled" test: Read the sentence aloud. If it sounds like it was built from a template: subject → verb → object with no variation, three+ sentences in identical grammatical structure, vocabulary technically correct but no person would say it naturally — it fails Human Voice.

#### Pass B — Surgical Rewrite
Fix every item on the Pass A failure list. Only those items. Do not rewrite passing sentences.

**Eight rewriting rules:**
1. **Start variation** — No two consecutive sentences start with the same word. After fixing, check the sentence before and after.
2. **Number grounding** — Every vague claim gets a specific verified number from the source document. No estimates.
3. **Sentence length** — After rewriting, count words. Over 25: split. Two ideas joined by "and" or "but": test two sentences. Usually stronger.
4. **Active voice** — Find the agent of the action. Make it the subject.
5. **One idea per sentence** — Comma separating two independent clauses: test whether each is stronger alone.
6. **Sentence rhythm** — Read aloud after fixes. Short sentences punch. Long sentences explain. Three+ sentences the same length: vary one.
7. **Buyer language** — Use the words buyers use. "Gate opener" not "access automation system." "Opens and closes" not "cycles."
8. **No urgency language** — Homeowners research gate openers over days or weeks. Urgency language reads as manipulation. Remove without replacement.

If any failure cannot be fixed because required spec data does not exist, note what data is needed from the client and remove the claim.

#### Pass D — GEO & E-E-A-T Check

Run after Pass B. Six binary checks — pass or fail, no partial credit.

| Check | Pass condition |
|---|---|
| AI-quotable paragraph | Present in opening section. Self-contained. Contains brand name, product category, one spec, one differentiator. |
| FAQ coverage | All 8 required topics answered. Each answer 3+ sentences. Each answer spec-anchored. |
| Comparison FAQ | One FAQ directly compares Open Bear to the correct competitor for the product type. Spec-to-spec, no vague claims. |
| Entity clarity block | Brand name, product category, use case, and differentiator all appear within first 100 words. |
| Expertise signals | Correct technical terms used. Every term explained inline on first use. At least one spec with the "why it matters" bridge. |
| Authority signals | At least 2 mandatory authority statements present. CE cert appears only on Side-Mounted Swing. No unverified authority claims. |

**Any fail = fix before moving to Pass C.**

#### Pass C — Consumer Conversion Review
Read the page as a homeowner who has never heard of Open Bear, comparing options, will leave if not convinced within 90 seconds.

**Six tests:**

**Test 1 — 3-second headline test**
Read only each section headline. Does it make a promise or state an outcome worth reading more about?
Failing: describes the section, names the product, uses adjectives without proof.
Passing: states an outcome, makes a promise, raises a question the next sentence answers.
Every failing headline: rewrite as an outcome statement of 5–8 words.

**Test 2 — First 10 words test**
Read only the first 10 words of each section's body copy. Do they state what the buyer gains?
First 10 words introducing the product, company, or a feature = fail.

**Test 3 — Objection coverage test**
Cross-check the full page against this list. Every objection must be answered with a specific, number-backed response:
- Electric shock risk · Gate weight handling · Climate performance · Power cut behaviour · Noise level · Phone control · Installation difficulty · Child and pet safety

**Test 4 — CTA journey test**
Map every CTA. Each must advance the reader one step closer to a decision. No two CTAs to the same destination on one page.
- Early page: orientation (find the right model)
- Mid page: verification (download spec, compare)
- End of page: conversion (get a quote)

**Test 5 — Differentiation test**
Read the page as a buyer who has also seen a competitor's page. At least 2 of 5 differentiators must appear explicitly:
- DC24V safety voltage — safe to touch, no electric shock risk
- Servo motor — smooth, load-aware, no slamming
- Factory-direct — buying from the manufacturer, no reseller markup
- 50,000 units/year production capacity
- -35°C to 70°C operating range

**Test 6 — Read-aloud test**
Read the full page aloud. Mark every hesitation, every sentence read twice, every phrase that sounds written not spoken. Every marked sentence is a rewrite.

**AI-Written Score — target 15% or below**

Signals that raise the score (+5% each):
- Three+ consecutive sentences in identical grammatical structure
- "not only... but also" construction
- Transitions: Furthermore · Additionally · Moreover · In conclusion · It is worth noting
- Perfect parallel bullet points (every bullet same length and structure)
- Any CTA applicable to any product on any website
- Vocabulary technically correct but no person would naturally say it

Signals that lower the score (-5% each):
- Intentional sentence fragment for punch
- Sentence starting with "And" or "But"
- A specific non-round number
- Direct mid-paragraph address to the reader
- An unexpected analogy grounding an abstract claim
- A rhetorical question answered immediately by the next sentence

If above 15%: identify top 3 AI-signal sentences, rewrite, reassess. Maximum 2 loops.

## 8. GEO / AI SEO Rules

> These rules make Open Bear content citable by AI search engines — Perplexity, ChatGPT, Gemini, and Google AI Overviews. AI pulls direct, specific, self-contained answers. Content that hedges, vagues out, or requires context gets skipped.

### The AI-Quotable Paragraph
Every product page must contain one paragraph that passes this test: an AI engine could lift it verbatim and it would make complete sense with zero surrounding context.

**Structure:**
```
[Brand] [Product type] [specific capability] — [key spec] — [use case] — [differentiator].
```

**Example (passing):**
> Open Bear's sliding gate operator handles gates up to 1,800 kg with 650 Nm of torque. It runs on a 24V DC servo motor — safe to touch mid-cycle — and operates from -35°C to 70°C without modification. Factory-direct pricing means no reseller markup.

**Example (failing):**
> Our gate operators are designed for performance and reliability in all conditions. We offer a range of models to suit different needs.

Place this paragraph in the opening section of every product page. It doubles as the schema description field.

---

### FAQ Standards — Mandatory Rules

FAQs are the highest GEO-value section on any page. AI search engines treat FAQ content as a primary answer source.

#### Minimum per FAQ answer
- **Length:** 3–5 sentences minimum. One-liners will not be cited.
- **Self-contained:** The answer must make sense with no surrounding context — assume the reader lands on only that answer.
- **Spec-anchored:** Every answer must contain at least one verified number from `Reference/CLIENT-DATA-MAP.md`.
- **PAS structure preferred:** State the problem, confirm the risk, resolve with the spec.

#### Question format rules
- Write questions exactly as a person would type them into ChatGPT or Perplexity — not as formal headings
- Use full sentences: "What happens if the power cuts out?" not "Power failure"
- Include the product category in the question where it aids AI matching: "Can a sliding gate operator handle a 1,000 kg gate?"

#### Required FAQ topics — every product page must cover all eight
| Topic | Example question form |
|---|---|
| Weight / load capacity | "How heavy a gate can [product type] handle?" |
| Power cut behaviour | "What happens to my gate if the power goes out?" |
| Electric safety | "Is a 24V gate opener safe around children?" |
| Climate range | "Will this gate opener work in [extreme cold/heat]?" |
| Noise level | "How loud is [product type] during operation?" |
| Phone / remote control | "Can I open my gate from my phone?" |
| Installation | "How hard is it to install [product type]?" |
| Comparison | "How does Open Bear compare to [FAAC / LiftMaster / Nice]?" |

#### Comparison FAQ — mandatory on every product page
One FAQ answer must directly address the most searched competitor for that product category. Use the format:

```
Q: How does Open Bear [product] compare to [competitor] [product]?
A: [Spec-to-spec comparison. Open Bear differentiator called out explicitly. No vague claims.]
```

Competitor mapping (from `Reference/Keyword-Clusters/11-competitor-gap.md`):
- Sliding gate → FAAC, LiftMaster
- Swing gate → FAAC, Nice
- Barrier gate → BFT, FAAC

### Entity Clarity Block
Every page must establish four entity signals within the first 100 words:

| Entity signal | Example |
|---|---|
| Brand name | "Open Bear" — not "we" or "the company" |
| Product category | "sliding gate operator" — exact keyword match |
| Primary use case | "residential driveways and commercial entrances" |
| Key differentiator | "DC24V servo motor" or "factory-direct" |

This block feeds AI knowledge graph associations and improves brand entity disambiguation.

---

### Structured Answer Patterns
Use these sentence openers to signal direct answers to AI scrapers:

| Intent | Opener pattern |
|---|---|
| Capability | "[Product] handles / supports / operates..." |
| Safety | "Safe to touch — [reason + spec]" |
| Comparison | "Unlike [competitor], [Open Bear product] [specific difference]" |
| How-it-works | "When [trigger], the [component] [specific action]" |
| Spec confirmation | "[Spec] — confirmed on [product] — [context]" |

Avoid opener patterns that delay the answer: "It's important to note that...", "There are several factors...", "This depends on..."

## 9. E-E-A-T Standards

> E-E-A-T (Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, Trustworthiness) signals tell Google and AI engines that Open Bear content is written by people who know the product and can be trusted. Each signal must appear explicitly — Google cannot infer it.

### Experience Signals
Demonstrate real-world familiarity with the installation context and use case. Every product page must include at least two of the following:

| Signal type | Implementation |
|---|---|
| Real use case scenario | "A 600 kg timber driveway gate on a 5-metre span — the load the heavy-duty model was built for" |
| Installation context | "Ships complete — motor, controller, remotes, and all hardware. Nothing to source separately on site." |
| Failure mode addressed | "Most gate jams happen at peak load — the obstacle detection disengages under 150 N, well before mechanical damage occurs" |
| Comparison from experience | "Where rack-and-pinion drives wear unevenly under heavy loads, the servo motor adjusts torque in real time" |

Do not fabricate experience signals. Every scenario must be grounded in confirmed product behaviour from `Reference/CLIENT-DATA-MAP.md`.

---

### Expertise Signals
Use the correct technical language from the product manuals. Demonstrate mastery by explaining the why behind the spec, not just the spec itself.

**Expertise format:**
```
[Technical term] — [plain English] — [why it matters to the buyer]
```

> DC24V servo motor — runs at safe-touch voltage — which means even if a child contacts the motor housing mid-cycle, the current cannot cause a shock injury.

**Required technical terms per page type:**

| Page type | Required terms |
|---|---|
| Sliding gate product | Rack-and-pinion, duty cycle, torque (N·m), IP rating |
| Swing gate product | Torque arm, slow-start/slow-stop, F-class thermal protection |
| Barrier gate | Boom length, open time (seconds), loop detector |
| Category pages | At least 3 terms from the relevant product set |

Every technical term must be explained inline on first use — see Check 3A in Section 7.

---

### Authoritativeness Signals
Establish Open Bear as a credible manufacturer, not a reseller. These signals must appear on every product page.

**Mandatory authority statements:**

| Claim | Approved copy | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Manufacturing scale | "50,000 units produced annually" | CLIENT-DATA-MAP |
| CE certification | "CE certified" — Side-Mounted Swing only | Confirmed |
| Factory-direct | "Direct from the manufacturer — no reseller in the chain" | Business model |
| Operating since | "Gate automation products since 2018" — never "15+ years" | CLIENT-DATA-MAP |
| Documentation | "Full installation manual, wiring diagram, and parts list in every shipment" | Confirmed |

Do not add authority claims beyond this list. Unverified authority signals damage E-E-A-T.

**OEM credibility signal — product pages only:**
One sentence per product page must reference the product's engineering basis: servo motor type, drive mechanism, or control system standard. This signals original engineering knowledge, not reseller copy.

---

### Trustworthiness Signals
Every claim must be traceable. Readers — and AI engines — are increasingly trained to flag unverifiable claims.

**Trust rules:**

- Every spec number links back to `Reference/CLIENT-DATA-MAP.md` — if it is not there, remove it
- CE certification stated only on the Side-Mounted Swing Door Operator — nowhere else
- No testimonials, star ratings, or review counts unless real data is supplied by client
- No "industry-leading", "most trusted", "top-rated" without a verifiable source
- Warranty terms: only state what is confirmed — do not imply coverage that is not confirmed
- Company founding: 2015 (business), 2018 (gate automation) — use specific year, never "over a decade"

**Trust-negative patterns to remove on sight:**
- "Trusted by thousands of homeowners" — unverified count
- "Award-winning" — no award confirmed
- "Guaranteed satisfaction" — not a confirmed policy
- Any stock-photo-style lifestyle claim not tied to a product spec

## 9. What NOT to Do

- Never invent specs. Every number from `Reference/CLIENT-DATA-MAP.md` or spec PDFs.
- Never claim certifications unless confirmed. CE cert on Side-Mounted Swing only.
- Never write "We offer..." — second person or third person only.
- Never run three-pass system on individual sections — Tier 1 per section, three passes on full compiled page only.
- Never float spec numbers as standalone labels — embed in sentences.
- Never skip the consumer journey read before passes — structural fixes here are cheaper.
- Never write two CTAs in the same section.
- Never use urgency language for this audience.
- Never present a section to the user before running all five Tier 1 checks.
